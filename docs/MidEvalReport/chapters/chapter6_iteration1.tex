\section{Iteration Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A live borrowing lifecycle in which invalid transitions and double bookings cannot be stored, and every state can be rebuilt from events.
\item The assurance engine on real evidence: outcomes from closed loans, the recency-weighted posterior, indirect evidence, manipulation signals and the decision policy, with an explanation on every result.
\item The human loop: disputes, holds and moderator decisions, with a resolved dispute feeding back into evidence.
\item Evidence that the engine behaves as designed: the mid-term scenarios in CI, and $k$-hop latency and coverage measured.
\item A pipeline in which every change is checked before merge and \code{main} is deployed automatically.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Algorithms and Technical Decisions}
\label{sec:algorithms}
The four algorithms below are implemented as specified in the Part~A algorithm and data-structure design. All parameter values quoted are those of the current configuration, version \code{\ConfigVersion}.

\subsection{Algorithm 1: recency-weighted Beta posterior (S2)}
Each confirmed outcome $i$ of the borrower in the borrower role is a success ($y_i=1$) or a failure ($y_i=0$) for one metric. An outcome $d_i$ days old with similarity $\sigma_i$ to the request has weight
\[ w_i = 2^{-d_i/h}\,\max(\sigma_{\min}, \sigma_i), \qquad h = 180 \text{ days},\ \sigma_{\min}=0.5, \]
where $\sigma_i = 0.4\,c_{\text{band}} + 0.4\,[\text{same category}] + 0.2\,\frac{\text{shorter duration}}{\text{longer duration}}$ and $c_\text{band}$ is 1, 0.5 or 0 for the same, an adjacent or a distant value band (D17). With $s=\sum w_i y_i$ and $f=\sum w_i(1-y_i)$ the posterior is $\mathrm{Beta}(1+s,\,1+f)$; the engine reports its mean, the 5\% and 95\% quantiles (the 90\% credible interval) and the effective evidence $n_\mathrm{eff}=s+f$. With no history this is $\mathrm{Beta}(1,1)$: mean 0.5, interval $[0.05, 0.95]$, which the policy reports as INSUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE, not as a low value. \emph{Check values tested}: weights 0.962 and 0.25 at 10 and 360 days; 18 successes and 2 failures give $\mathrm{Beta}(19,3)$. \emph{Complexity}: $O(n)$ time for $n$ outcomes, $O(1)$ extra space. \emph{Why}: a conjugate posterior gives an honest interval from very few observations \cite{josang2002beta}, and the half-life lets old behaviour fade without being forgotten.

\subsection{Algorithm 2: bounded $k$-hop indirect evidence (S1)}
Trust edges are derived only from verified outcomes (ADR-0006): the weight of edge $(a,b)$ is the posterior mean of $a$'s verified outcomes with $b$. For a lender $u$ and borrower $v$:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Find simple paths from $u$ to $v$ of at most $k=3$ edges (depth-first, no repeated node, so cycles are safe).
\item Score a path $P$ with $h$ edges as $q(P)=\gamma^{\,h-1}\prod_{e\in P} w_e$, with $\gamma=0.7$ (the first hop is not penalised).
\item Keep only the strongest path per first-hop neighbour; paths through the same neighbour are not independent evidence.
\item Combine with noisy-OR: $I = 1-\prod_{P}(1-q(P))$.
\item Reliability $\rho=\min\!\big(0.6,\ \min(1,|P|/3)\cdot\min(1,|F|/2)\cdot I\big)$, where $|F|$ is the number of distinct first hops.
\item Indirect estimate $t_\text{ind}=b+\rho\,(t_v-b)$, with population baseline $b=0.5$.
\end{enumerate}
The cap $\rho\leq 0.6$ keeps indirect evidence below direct evidence, and the policy never lets $\rho$ alone produce SUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE. \emph{Complexity}: $O\big(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta^i\big)$ for maximum out-degree $\Delta$. On the hand-worked 8-node graph the code reproduces the designed values ($I=0.7164$, $\rho=0.6$ capped; $\rho=0, 0.424, 0.600, 0.600$ for $k=1..4$). \emph{Why}: trust that weakens with distance follows Golbeck \cite{golbeck2005computing}; the cap, the path-diversity rule and the bounded $k$ are the project's adaptations for small, dense neighbourhood graphs.

\paragraph{$k$ sweep.}\label{sec:khop-results} Table~\ref{tab:ksweep} reports latency and coverage for $k=1$ to 4 on the synthetic \emph{scale} tier (2{,}026 users, 5{,}987 edges, seed 20261001), one search per (lender, borrower) pair of 2{,}000 loans. $k=3$ finds a path for 93.2\% of pairs at 4.5~ms (P95); $k=4$ adds one percentage point of coverage at 3.5 times the P95 latency and 7 times the maximum, so $k=3$ is kept. The sweep was measured with configuration 1.2.0; version 1.3.0 changed only manipulation-detection parameters, so the $k$-hop results still apply. \emph{Caveat:} the pairs are existing loans, so a direct edge between lender and borrower usually exists already, and the $k=1$ coverage (80.3\%) includes the assessed loan's own edge. This overstates coverage for genuinely new pairs; the FYP-2 evaluation uses time-ordered held-out pairs instead.

\subsection{Algorithm 3: manipulation-risk scoring (S2)}
Manipulation risk is a separate 0--100 dimension, never averaged with trust. Iteration~1 computes two signals and one check over a 90-day window:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{S1, partner clustering (D18).} Take the borrower's review partners (at least 3). Let $r$ be the share of pairs of partners who reviewed each other in both directions; $S_1=\mathrm{clip}\big((r-0.3)/0.6\big)$. A ring of accounts fills almost every pair; an ordinary borrower's lenders rarely review one another. The simpler ``A rated B and B rated A'' rule was rejected because every loan produces two reviews: on the standard tier it flagged 6 of 8 ordinary members; partner clustering flags 0 of 9.
\item \textbf{S4, similar review texts.} The reviews the borrower received, as character 3-grams weighted by TF-IDF; the highest cosine similarity $c$ between two of them gives $S_4=\mathrm{clip}\big((c-0.7)/0.25\big)$.
\item \textbf{Retaliation check (D19).} A low review (lowest rating $\leq 2$) posted within 14 days after the subject complained about its author, about a loan whose return was confirmed, is set aside: left out of S1 and S4, reported, and explained to the lender in neutral words.
\end{itemize}
The score is the weighted mean over the available signals, $\text{score} = 100\cdot\sum_i w_i s_i \big/ \sum_i w_i$ (with $w_{S1}=0.3$, $w_{S4}=0.2$; a signal without enough data is left out of both sums), banded into levels 1 to 4 at 20, 40 and 60. Level~4 additionally requires two signals $\geq 0.5$ (ADR-0007); otherwise the result is level~3. A legitimate family group therefore stays at level~3 (TC-38): extra verification, no hold. \emph{Complexity}: S1 $O(p^2)$ for $p$ partners; S4 $O(m^2L)$ for $m$ reviews of length $L$. \emph{Why}: collusion shows as groups that rate one another \cite{mukherjee2012spotting,hooi2016fraudar}, while two-sided reviews are reciprocal by nature \cite{bolton2013engineering,fradkin2021reciprocity}. \emph{Known limit}: a ring that also trades with outsiders lowers its own density (camouflage).

The first rule whose condition holds decides (Table~\ref{tab:rules}). Thresholds (D12): $\tau_\mu=0.75$, $\tau_L=0.60$, $n_{\min}=3$, $\rho_\mathrm{sup}=0.3$.

Because R7 and R8 need $n_\mathrm{eff}\geq 3$ from the borrower's own outcomes, indirect evidence alone can never yield SUFFICIENT\_EVIDENCE (property-tested, TC-34). Each rule maps to a policy decision and a set of safeguards (deposit record, handover photos and checklist, fixed return date, verification), and the explanation names the rule, the interval, the effective evidence, the community paths and the signals. The database refuses an assessment row whose rule, state, decision and hold reason disagree (\code{assessment\_rule\_ck}). \emph{Complexity}: $O(1)$ given the inputs; a whole assessment is $O(n)+O(\Delta^k)+O(p^2+m^2L)$.

\subsection{Other technical decisions}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Engine timeout.} The lifecycle calls the engine in a worker with a 2{,}000~ms timeout from the configuration; a timeout or exception holds the request with \code{engine\_failure} and stores no assurance state.
\item \textbf{Moderator decisions.} \code{plan()} turns a decision into exactly one lifecycle event (\code{dispute\_resolved}, \code{hold\_cleared} or \code{request\_declined}) according to the hold reason, and refuses invalid combinations, such as safeguards on an engine-failure hold.
\item \textbf{Strict request bodies.} Every request schema refuses unknown fields and NUL characters, so a client cannot inject \code{actor\_id}, \code{authorisation} or \code{new\_state}.
\end{itemize}
